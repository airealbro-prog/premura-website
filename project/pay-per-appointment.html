The Two Ways to Buy Appointments

Renting appointments
vs owning the setter

Every appointment-setting offer in solar and roofing is one of these two models. They behave very differently once you run them for a quarter.

Pay per appointment

You buy the output

  • $150–$300 per appointment, setup fees common
  • Often non-exclusive — the same homeowner can be sold to multiple companies
  • No-show and refund policies vary; the effective cost of a sat appointment runs higher than the sticker
  • Zero monthly commitment — and zero compounding: the vendor's callers never learn your pitch
  • Volume is whatever the vendor has this week

Dedicated agent

You buy the machine that makes them

  • Priced per dedicated agent per month — final pricing depends on team size and campaign length
  • 100% exclusive — your agent calls only your territory, books only your calendar
  • 5+ qualified appointments per agent per week is the floor we manage to
  • Compounds: the agent learns your territory, your objections, your offer
  • Scale by adding agents, not by hoping the vendor has inventory

Straight Answers

Pay-per-appointment questions
we get on every call

How much does pay-per-appointment solar or roofing lead generation cost?

Most programs charge between $150 and $300 per appointment, and setup fees are common. Many are not exclusive — the same homeowner can be sold to more than one company — and refund policies for no-shows vary widely, so the effective cost per appointment that actually sits is usually higher than the sticker price.

What does Premura charge instead?

We price per dedicated agent, not per appointment. Pricing depends on how many agents you run and for how long. Each trained agent books 5+ qualified appointments per week into your calendar, and everything above the floor costs nothing extra. No contracts are required to start the conversation.

Are the appointments exclusive?

Yes. Your agent calls only your territory, follows your qualification criteria, and books directly into your closers' calendars. Appointments are never resold or shared — which is the most common complaint we hear from operators coming off pay-per-appointment programs.

When does pay-per-appointment actually make sense?

If you need fewer than roughly 8–10 appointments a month, are testing a brand-new territory, or need to fill a short-term gap with zero commitment — buy them one at a time. Once you consistently need 10+, the math flips: a dedicated agent producing 20+ a month costs less per appointment, is exclusive, and compounds as the agent learns your market.
