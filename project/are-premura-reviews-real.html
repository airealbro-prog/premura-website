The Fair Suspicion

Assume every review
online might be fake

That is a healthy instinct, and we would rather you keep it than switch it off for us.

Anyone can generate a wall of five-star ratings. Anyone can paste a glowing paragraph next to a stock headshot. Because that is so easy, a pile of anonymous praise proves almost nothing. The only reviews worth trusting are the ones you can trace back to a real, named person who is willing to be identified. That is the standard we hold our own testimonials to, and the standard we would encourage you to hold every agency to, including us.

A Checklist You Can Reuse

How to vet any
agency's reviews in ten minutes

This works on us and on every competitor you are comparing. Run it on all of them.

  1. Look for names and faces, not initials

    Real testimonials come from identifiable people. Initials, first-name-only text quotes, and stock photography are the classic markers of reviews that cannot be traced back to anyone.

  2. Prefer video and detail over one-line blurbs

    A five-word rave is worthless. A person walking through what they tried, what changed, and their actual numbers is much harder to fabricate and much more useful to you.

  3. Check the results for specifics

    Vague superlatives like best ever should raise your guard. Concrete, checkable figures like a number of appointments per month are the kind of claim a real operator makes.

  4. Confirm the company exists off its own website

    Find a third-party record. Premura Corp has a Better Business Bureau profile, a Florida registration, a published phone number, and a support email. A company that is hard to verify anywhere but its own homepage is a warning sign.

  5. Ask to see real data or talk to a real client

    The strongest test is whether a company will let you look behind the marketing. We show current clients on a live dashboard, and you can ask about it directly on a call.
