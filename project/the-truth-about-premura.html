Where The Confusion Comes From

What critics get right,
and what they get wrong

Skepticism about outbound calling is not baseless. Here is an honest look at the criticism, split into the fair part and the mistaken part.

What critics get right

The fair points we agree with

  • The outbound space has overpromisers, so caution is warranted
  • This is a paid service, and it only pays off if you can close appointments
  • Results depend on your territory, your offer, and your sales team
  • No provider should promise guaranteed, effortless, overnight outcomes

What critics get wrong

The mismatched assumptions

  • Premura is confused with pay-per-lead vendors. It is not. Agents are dedicated and exclusive to your territory
  • It is assumed to be a lead app. It is a managed call center of real, trained people
  • It is assumed to be opaque. Clients watch a live dashboard, not a monthly summary
  • It is assumed to trap you. There is no long-term lock-in to start

Most criticism traces back to mismatched expectations rather than misconduct. When someone expects free, guaranteed leads and instead finds a paid, managed service that requires a closing team, the gap gets described as a complaint. Set the expectation correctly and the picture changes.

What is the truth about Premura?

Premura is a real, registered Florida corporation, Premura Corp, founded in September 2022, that builds and manages dedicated in-house call centers for solar, roofing, and home-services companies. It has a public address in Medley, FL, a published phone number, a BBB profile, and named clients on camera. It is a paid, done-for-you service.

Why is there confusion or criticism about Premura online?

Outbound calling attracts skepticism because some providers in the space overpromise or sell shared leads. Premura also gets confused with pay-per-lead vendors, which it is not. It provides dedicated agents exclusive to your territory. Most criticism comes from mismatched expectations rather than misconduct.

Do critics have a point?

The fair point is that this is a paid service that only works when the client can close appointments and commit to a dedicated team. Where critics get it wrong is assuming it is a lead app or a guaranteed result. It is a managed call center with real people and a live dashboard.
