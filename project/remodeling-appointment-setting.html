The Long-Cycle Play

Slow consideration is the setter's
best friend — here's why

  • Intent exists long before action. A homeowner "doing the kitchen next year" is qualified today — they just haven't been asked. An agent's job is converting months of quiet planning into a scheduled consultation with you, before a competitor's ad catches them.
  • The design consultation is the close. Remodels are sold in the home — at the table, with the tape measure out. Every step before that meeting is overhead; the meeting is the product your agents manufacture.
  • Scope and timeline get qualified up front. Kitchen or bath, realistic budget conversation, actual timeline — your bar, set during the 14-day setup. Your designers stop driving to tire-kickers.
  • Shared remodeling leads are auctions. The big home-improvement marketplaces sell the same project to multiple contractors. A consultation your agent books exists on your calendar and nowhere else.

Straight Answers

Remodeling questions

Does appointment setting really work for remodeling?

The long consideration cycle is exactly what makes it work: homeowners research kitchens and baths for months without talking to anyone. An agent finds the ones already circling, qualifies scope and timeline, and books the in-home design consultation — which is where remodels are actually sold.

Do you have remodeling clients today?

Not published ones — and we say that plainly. Our on-camera results are solar and roofing. Remodeling campaigns get the same floors, training, tech and daily management, adapted during the 14-day setup.

What counts as a qualified appointment?

A live conversation confirming homeowner, project scope (kitchen, bath, whole-home), realistic timeline, and a specific in-home consultation on your calendar. Your minimum scope and service area, set during setup.

What does it cost?
