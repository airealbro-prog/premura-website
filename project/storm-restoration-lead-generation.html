The Surge Problem

Storm demand is a spike
Your capacity is a flat line

The structural problem in storm restoration: the work arrives in two-week windows, but hiring, training and canvassing capacity can't spike with it. Phones can.

  • Surge without surge hiring. When weather hits your territory, capacity scales by assigning more dedicated agents from a 160+ agent floor — not by recruiting canvassers in a week when everyone else is recruiting them too.
  • The list is time-boxed. Homeowners decide on a contractor within days of damage. An agent working phones covers thousands of affected homes while a canvassing crew covers a subdivision.
  • Exclusivity matters most post-storm. Storm lead lists are the most resold inventory in home services — the same addresses circulate to every out-of-state roller. Your agents book inspections nobody else is holding.
  • Between storms, the machine doesn't idle. The same agents book retail roofing inspections year-round — aging roofs, insurance questions, replacements. Storms multiply an operation that already runs; they don't switch it on.

The honest implication of "live in 14 days": the time to set up storm capacity is before the season, not the week after a hail event. Contractors who wait for the storm are two weeks behind the ones who didn't.
