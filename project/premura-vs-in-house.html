Premura vs building in-house
The honest comparison, both ways

You have two real options for outbound appointment setting: hire and manage your own setters, or have Premura run a dedicated team for you. Both can work. This page lays out the trade-offs candidly, including when building your own is genuinely the better call, so you can pick with your eyes open.

The Real Question

It is not talent
it is who runs the machine

Anyone can hire a few people and buy a dialer. The hard part of an outbound team is not the pieces, it is running them together, every day, until they produce. Building in-house means you own that job. With Premura, we own it and you own the results. The right choice comes down to whether you want a calling operation to be one of the things your company is great at, or whether you just want a full calendar this quarter.

Honest framing: this is not a case for never building in-house. It is a case for choosing deliberately instead of defaulting into a hiring spree you have not scoped.

The honest cut: if outbound calling is going to be a permanent, large-scale part of your company and you want the team as an owned asset, building in-house eventually wins. If what you want is a full calendar without running a call center, Premura is the machine that already runs.

The Part People Underestimate

What building in-house
actually asks of you

The dialer subscription is one small piece. These are the jobs that come with owning the team, the ones that fill your week whether you planned for them or not.

  1. Recruiting and surviving turnover

    Cold calling has brutal washout. You are not hiring two setters, you are hiring several to keep two, and the churn never fully stops. That pipeline is now yours to run.

  2. Writing a script that survives real homeowners

    The first draft never works. Scripts get good through hundreds of iterations against live objections. In-house, every one of those iterations is on your time.

  3. Buying and feeding the tech

    A dialer, contact data, and a CRM that syncs to a calendar are separate purchases you configure and integrate. Premura bundles these, with data generated in-house and refreshed weekly.

  4. Managing the floor every single day

    This is the piece that sinks most DIY builds. Call centers decay without daily coaching, listening to calls, and cutting what does not work. A weekly check-in is not management. With Premura, setters dial 7 to 8 hours a day under daily coaching, and you watch it on a live dashboard. See how it works.

Straight Answers

Premura vs in-house
Common questions

Is it cheaper to build my own in-house setters or use Premura?

It depends on scale and on whether you count management time. Building in-house means paying recruiting, salaries, dialer, data, and CRM as separate costs, plus a manager who runs the floor every day. Premura bundles all of that into a per dedicated agent price. At permanent, large scale, in-house can win on unit cost. To get appointments quickly without running a second company, Premura is usually the faster path.

How long does building an in-house team take versus Premura?

Building in-house realistically takes months of hiring, testing callers, iterating a script, configuring a dialer and CRM, and setting up compliance. Premura is faster to results because the floor, the training program, and the tech stack already exist, so a dedicated agent starts dialing your territory without you building any of that first.

Who manages the callers with Premura?

Premura does. Agents are recruited, trained, and coached daily by Premura, and replaced until they perform. Campaigns are monitored every day rather than reviewed in a monthly report, and you watch results on a live dashboard while talking to your agents directly. In-house, that daily management job falls on you.

Do I lose control if Premura runs it instead of building in-house?

No. The agents are dedicated to you, calling your territory with a script and QA standards you approve. You pick the zip codes, you sign off on the script, and appointments book straight into your own calendar. You direct the campaign without having to hire, train, or manage the floor yourself.

When does building in-house make more sense?

Building in-house makes more sense when outbound calling is going to be a permanent, large-scale core competency of your company and you want the team as an owned asset. If you plan to run many seats indefinitely and want full control of every piece, an in-house build can eventually win on cost and ownership.
