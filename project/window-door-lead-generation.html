The High-Ticket Math

One closed consultation
pays for the month

Window and door projects are big-ticket. That changes the arithmetic of appointment setting completely.

  • The consultation is the product. A window deal has no phone close and no online checkout — a demo in the home is the only path. That makes calendar volume the single number your revenue follows.
  • High tickets forgive the setter's cost instantly. An agent averaging $2,500/month who books 20+ consultations needs exactly one of them to close for the math to work — everything after is margin.
  • Purchased home-improvement leads are the worst of their kind. The big lead marketplaces sell the same homeowner to multiple contractors, and your closer arrives third. A dedicated agent books homeowners who agreed to a time with you — nobody else is coming.
  • Scope is qualified before it's booked. How many windows, which problem — drafts, noise, security, curb appeal — and homeowner confirmed. Your criteria, set during the 14-day setup.

What makes a consultation "qualified"?

A live conversation with the homeowner confirming ownership, scope (how many windows or doors, what problem), and agreement to a specific in-home time — landed directly on your closer's calendar. Your criteria, set during setup.

Are consultations exclusive?

Structurally yes — your agent calls only your territory and books only your calendar. Nothing exists in a marketplace to resell, unlike the big home-improvement lead platforms.

What does it cost?

Per dedicated agent — averaging $2,500 per agent per month — with 5+ qualified consultations per week as the managed floor. On window-and-door ticket sizes, one closed consultation typically covers the month.
