Worksheet · 03

Why the Number Is Stuck

On paid ads, quantity and quality are the same dial. One targeting setting controls both, so every time you fix one you break the other. On outbound they are two separate dials.

One dial

Paid ads and bought appointments

Widen the targeting and volume goes up while quality falls. Tighten it and quality goes up while volume collapses — and your cost per appointment climbs, because the same spend now buys fewer of them. There is one control and it moves both numbers at once.

  • Want more appointments? Loosen targeting — and sit with worse ones.
  • Want better appointments? Tighten targeting — and get fewer.
  • Either way the per-appointment cost moves against you.
Two dials

A dedicated outbound call center

Volume and quality are set in different places by different people. Volume is a function of how much dialling happens. Quality is decided in the conversation, by what the agent refuses to book. Turning one does not move the other.

Volume dial
  • Dials made per agent, per day
  • How much of your territory is being worked
  • Agents on the campaign — add one, add its appointments
Quality dial
  • How hard the agent qualifies before booking
  • Homeowner, roof or bill, decision-maker present, timeframe
  • What gets turned away instead of booked

This is why the two figures you just worked out can be so far apart. A cheap appointment nobody sits is not cheap, and tightening a targeting dial to fix that makes your cost per appointment worse. Qualifying harder on a call costs you nothing but the call.
